## Why every citation got pulled and read, not reused from the packet

The task brief flagged that several earlier items in this notes-writing series were blocked
because a citation was topically adjacent to a claim but didn't actually back its specific
content. This packet's own `current_cite` field for all 30 questions is just
`"tablica ODGOVORI NA PITANJA, redak N, stupac Skupina III"` — the answer-key table location,
not a real content citation — so there was nothing reusable to begin with; every citation in
the note file was built from scratch by reading the actual RK book prose with `booktool.py`.

For every one of the 30 ids I:
1. Rendered the actual printed page (pixmap, not just OCR text — this book's line art is
   ~950 one-pixel inline images invisible to `get_images()`/`get_drawings()`) for the 11
   questions that carry a figure (`bk-teh-3-01/03/05/09/19/21/43/45/47/51/53`), and confirmed
   the figure's actual content (not just trusted `has_figure: false` in the packet, which is
   wrong for all 11 — they do carry figures).
2. Found the specific book section/formula/worked-example the question is drawn from, using
   `booktool.py find` plus reading the surrounding pages directly, and confirmed the citation's
   text actually produces the HRS-marked answer by computation or verbatim match — not just a
   plausible nearby topic.
3. Compared every option's exact text against the raw page render (not just `book_parsed2.json`)
   for all 30 ids, since two earlier tehnicki packets (`bk-teh-2-03/13/29`) had swapped or
   merged-in options from adjacent questions. **No scrambling found in this packet** — all 30
   questions' stems, options, and answer letters match the printed page exactly.

## Notable finds while verifying

- **11 figures the packet metadata calls `has_figure: false`.** All 11 actually carry a figure
  central to the question (component networks, rectifier schematic, coax stub diagram, coupling
  curves, oscilloscope trace, block diagrams, filter circuits). Existing crops in
  `quiz/build/figs_book/bk-teh-3-*.png` from `book_overrides.json` made these directly viewable;
  I read all 11 as images before writing the note.
- **Three figures are verbatim reused from the book's own descriptive chapters, not new exam
  art**: `bk-teh-3-21`'s coupling curves (labels A/B/C, 460-470 kHz) are the *exact same figure*
  as book section 3.2.2 (str. 110), which already names curve B "slaba sprega" and curve C
  "tijesna sprega" — the exam question just asks the reader to recall the book's own labels.
  `bk-teh-3-51`'s antenna-coupler figure reuses the exact labels ("Antena s napajanjem na
  kraju", "Prema odašiljaču") from Sl. 9.2.2 (str. 193). `bk-teh-3-45`'s block diagram
  (Konvertor/Logički sklop/Sat/Brojilo/Prikaz) matches Sl. 8.2.3 (str. 183) block-for-block.
- **`bk-teh-3-13`'s stem lost a λ symbol to OCR**: "odrezan je na el. duljinu i za frekvenciju od
  100 MHz" is missing the wavelength symbol between "duljinu" and "i". I did not edit the
  packet/parsed text (out of scope), but the note explains the gap and shows that reading it as
  "el. duljinu λ" is the only interpretation that reproduces the HRS answer (λ=3m at 100MHz,
  ×0,66 velocity factor = 1,98m, matching option a exactly) — flagged as an OCR gap, not
  invented as fact.
- **`bk-teh-3-43` required deriving a number from the adjacent even-numbered question's
  figure** (`bk-teh-3-42`, not in this packet — it's part of the `_b` packet). Its own text
  says "signal prikazan u zadatku 42", so I rendered that oscilloscope figure directly: reading
  the grid (3 divisions × 3V/div = 9V peak, 8 divisions × 1ms/div = 8ms period) and computing
  P=Uef²/R=(9/√2)²/50≈0,81W independently reproduces the HRS answer "približno 0,8W" — I did not
  assume or assert what Q42's own marked answer is (out of scope), only read the shared figure.
- No HRS-vs-current-law conflicts found or flagged in this packet — it is pure electronics/RF
  theory (components, circuits, receivers, transmitters, antennas, propagation, measurements,
  interference, safety), not propisi/pravila, so the currency-audit flag vocabulary (`check`/
  `conflict`) does not apply here; nothing needed flagging under rule 1.
